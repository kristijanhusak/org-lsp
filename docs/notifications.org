#+title: Notifications
#+options: ^:{} toc:1

Reminders of the day's appointments, with no editor open, as Emacs's
~appt.el~ gives them once ~org-agenda-to-appt~ has made the appointments
of the agenda files. ~org-lsp notify~ looks at the agenda files and sends
the reminders due that minute; the system runs it each minute (cron, a
systemd timer, launchd, the Task Scheduler). It keeps nothing between runs.

#+begin_example
Appointment in 12 minutes
10:00 TODO Standup                            :meeting:
#+end_example

* Setting it up
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: setting-it-up
:END:

#+begin_src sh
scripts/install.sh notifications
# or, without a checkout:
curl -fsSL https://github.com/kristijanhusak/org-lsp/releases/download/nightly/install.sh | bash -s -- notifications
# and to take it away again:
scripts/install.sh --remove notifications
#+end_src

The server goes in =~/.local/bin= first if it is not there
(~ORG_LSP_BIN_DIR~ for another directory). The agenda files are those of
the shared configuration file, =~/.config/org-lsp/config.toml= (~org-lsp
config~ shows which and what it says):

#+begin_src toml
org_agenda_files = ["~/org"]
#+end_src

What the script makes, which can be made by hand as well:

- Linux, with systemd :: a user timer,
  =~/.config/systemd/user/org-lsp-notify.timer=, that runs
  =org-lsp-notify.service= at the start of each minute:

  #+begin_src conf
  [Service]
  Type=oneshot
  ExecStart="/home/me/.local/bin/org-lsp" notify

  [Timer]
  OnCalendar=*-*-* *:*:00
  AccuracySec=1s
  #+end_src

  ~systemctl --user list-timers~ shows it, ~journalctl --user -u
  org-lsp-notify~ what it said. ~notify-send~ needs the session's bus: if
  no notification comes, ~systemctl --user import-environment DISPLAY
  WAYLAND_DISPLAY DBUS_SESSION_BUS_ADDRESS~ in the session's startup gives
  the timer what it needs.
- Linux, without systemd :: a line of the crontab (~crontab -l~), added
  once:

  #+begin_src sh
  ,* * * * * DBUS_SESSION_BUS_ADDRESS=unix:path=/run/user/1000/bus '/home/me/.local/bin/org-lsp' notify
  #+end_src

  Cron gives its commands little of the session: the address of the
  session's bus is there for ~notify-send~. ~1000~ is your user's number
  (~id -u~).
- macOS :: a LaunchAgent, =~/Library/LaunchAgents/org.org-lsp.notify.plist=,
  that runs ~org-lsp notify~ at the start of each minute (a
  ~StartCalendarInterval~ with no fields), loaded with ~launchctl
  bootstrap~. The notifications are those of [[https://github.com/julienXX/terminal-notifier][terminal-notifier]] when it is
  installed (~brew install terminal-notifier~; looked for on the ~PATH~
  and in Homebrew's =bin= directories), with the logo, else of
  ~osascript~ (Script Editor's in System Settings > Notifications), which
  shows no image of its own.
- Windows :: a scheduled task, ~org-lsp-notify~, each minute
  (~schtasks /SC MINUTE~). It runs
  =%LOCALAPPDATA%\org-lsp\notify.vbs= with ~wscript~, which starts
  ~org-lsp.exe notify~ without a window. The notifications are toasts of
  [[https://github.com/Windos/BurntToast][BurntToast]] when that PowerShell module is installed, else of
  Windows's own, in the name of PowerShell.

Elsewhere, run ~org-lsp notify~ each minute in any way the system has.

** The icon
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-icon
:END:

A desktop notification shows the org-lsp logo. The server holds it, and
writes it, the first time it shows a notification, to =org-lsp/icon.png=
in the user's data directory, where the programs that show notifications
can read it: =$XDG_DATA_HOME=, else =~/.local/share=, on Linux,
=~/Library/Application Support= on macOS, =%LOCALAPPDATA%= on Windows.
Where it cannot be written, the notification comes without it.
~--dry-run~ writes nothing.

~notification_icon~ gives an image of one's own, or none:

#+begin_src toml
notification_icon = "~/pictures/bell.png"
# or no image:
notification_icon = ""
#+end_src

The image goes to ~notify-send --icon~, ~terminal-notifier -appIcon~,
BurntToast's ~-AppLogo~, or Windows's own toast as its picture.
~osascript~ cannot show one, whatever the setting says: install
~terminal-notifier~ for the logo on macOS.

* What it reminds of
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: what-it-reminds-of
:END:

The appointments are what ~org-agenda-to-appt~ takes with its defaults:
the entries of today's agenda that have a time of day, from a scheduled
date, a deadline or a timestamp, with repeaters and diary sexps in
timestamps on the days they fall; a time range at its start. Entries in a
done state are left out, as Org leaves out any whose text has a done
keyword and no other. ~scripts/appt_reference.py~ records the appointments
Emacs makes of the files of =tests/appt=, and the tests compare.

Each is reminded of ~appt_message_warning_time~ minutes before it (12),
then each ~appt_display_interval~ minutes (3) until it is due: at 9:48,
9:51, 9:54, 9:57 and 10:00 for a meeting at 10:00. An entry's
~APPT_WARNTIME~ property gives its own minutes. A list gives the minutes
of each reminder instead:

#+begin_src toml
appt_message_warning_time = [30, 5, 0]
#+end_src

Appointments due together come in one notification, as ~appt.el~ shows
them in one window: its mode line (~Appointment in 12 minutes~,
~Appointments in 5,10 minutes~, ~Appointment now~) is the title, and each
appointment a line of the text, its time and its headline, after the
minutes when they differ (~5m: 10:00 Standup~).

~notification_match~ keeps the entries a match string of the tags view
matches, as ~org-lsp agenda --view tags --match~ takes it:

#+begin_src toml
notification_match = "+meeting|+call"
#+end_src

** Warnings in hours
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: warnings-in-hours
:END:

With ~notification_use_timestamp_warning~, a deadline or timestamp with
a time and a warning cookie in hours (~-1h~, or ~--1h~) is reminded of
once that many hours before it as well, besides the reminders above
(~appt_message_warning_time~ or its ~APPT_WARNTIME~):

#+begin_src toml
notification_use_timestamp_warning = true
#+end_src

#+begin_src org
,* TODO Call Anna
DEADLINE: <2026-10-22 Thu 21:00 -1h>
#+end_src

#+begin_example
Appointment in 1 hour
21:00 TODO Call Anna
#+end_example

comes at 20:00, then the usual ones from 20:48. It is said as the other
reminders are, with the formats of [[#saying-it-your-way][Saying it your way]], and comes in one
notification with the appointments due that minute. A warning may fall
on the day before: ~<2026-10-23 Fri 01:00 -3h>~ is reminded of at 22:00
on the 22nd. A repeater's occurrence is reminded of on its day: a
timestamp repeats in the agenda when its repeater comes last
(~<2026-10-15 Thu 18:45 -1h +1w>~), and a repeating deadline is the
day's once the one before is done.

A scheduled date's cookie is a delay, which hides the entry until then,
not a warning: it is not reminded of. Cookies in days, weeks, months or
years (~-2d~) are left to the agenda and the deadlines of the day.

** The deadlines of the day
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-deadlines-of-the-day
:END:

With ~notification_deadline_summary_time~, once a day, at that time, one
notification lists the deadlines today's agenda shows that are not done:
due today, coming within their warning period (~org_deadline_warning_days~,
or the timestamp's own ~-3d~), and past, each with its category and the
agenda's words for it:

#+begin_src toml
notification_deadline_summary_time = "08:30"
#+end_src

#+begin_example
Deadlines
work: 2 d. ago: TODO Taxes
work: In 3 d.: TODO Report
#+end_example

No deadlines, no notification. ~notification_deadline_line_format~ writes
the lines otherwise ([[#saying-it-your-way][Saying it your way]]).

* Sending them elsewhere
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sending-them-elsewhere
:END:

~notification_command~ is a program and its arguments, run for each
notification in place of the desktop's, with ~%t~ for the title and ~%b~
for the text. No shell reads it. To a phone with [[https://ntfy.sh][ntfy]]:

#+begin_src toml
notification_command = ["ntfy", "publish", "--title", "%t", "my-org-reminders", "%b"]
#+end_src

or with ~curl~:

#+begin_src toml
notification_command = ["curl", "-fsS", "-H", "Title: %t", "--data-raw", "%b", "https://ntfy.sh/my-org-reminders"]
#+end_src

The program may read a value that starts an argument as one of its
options, or as more than text: a headline =-v= that ~%h~ puts there, or
one that starts with =@=, which ~curl -d~ takes for a file to send
(~--data-raw~ does not). Put the placeholder after an option that takes
it (~--title=%t~), or after other text (~<%h>~), where the program
allows.

With ~notification_desktop = true~ the desktop's comes too. A program that
fails, or takes longer than 30 seconds, is said on standard error, and the
command ends with status 1, as it does when an agenda file cannot be read
(the other files are reminded of all the same).

The command may say more of the entry with the placeholders of the next
section. With any of them it runs once for each appointment, with that
appointment's title, text and values, rather than once for the
appointments due together. To ntfy, the category as a tag:

#+begin_src toml
notification_command = ["ntfy", "publish", "--title", "%k %h", "--tags", "%c", "my-org-reminders", "%w %T"]
#+end_src

* Saying it your way
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: saying-it-your-way
:END:

~notification_title_format~ and ~notification_body_format~ write an
appointment's title and text from its entry, in place of ~appt.el~'s mode
line and line:

#+begin_src toml
notification_title_format = "%k %h"
notification_body_format = "%w %c %T"
#+end_src

#+begin_example
NEXT Review the plan
10:00 proj :home:work:meeting:
#+end_example

| Placeholder | Stands for                                                                  | Example                   |
|-------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+---------------------------|
| ~%h~        | the headline, without keyword, priority or tags; links by their description | =Review the plan=         |
| ~%k~        | the TODO keyword                                                            | =NEXT=                    |
| ~%p~        | the priority, empty without a cookie                                        | =A=                       |
| ~%T~        | the tags, inherited ones too when the agenda shows them                     | =:home:work:meeting:=     |
| ~%c~        | the category                                                                | =proj=                    |
| ~%s~        | the scheduled timestamp, as written                                         | =<2026-10-22 Thu 10:00>=  |
| ~%d~        | the deadline timestamp, as written                                          | =<2026-10-24 Sat>=        |
| ~%w~        | the appointment's time                                                      | =10:00=                   |
| ~%m~        | the minutes left                                                            | =10=                      |
| ~%e~        | the effort (~EFFORT~)                                                       | =0:30=                    |
| ~%f~        | the file's path                                                             | =/home/me/org/work.org=   |
| ~%F~        | the file's name                                                             | =work.org=                |
| ~%l~        | the headline's line in the file                                             | =7=                       |
| ~%a~        | the line ~appt.el~ shows, the default text                                  | =10:00 NEXT [#A] Review…= |
| ~%D~        | the deadline summary only: the agenda's leader                              | =In 2 d.:=                |
| ~%%~        | ~%~                                                                         |                           |

Any other ~%~ stays as it is; in ~notification_command~, ~%t~ and ~%b~
are the title and text. Appointments due together come in one
notification with the first one's title and a line of text each (the
minutes before the lines, as in ~5m: 10:00 Standup~, only with the
default text).

The deadline summary keeps its title; ~notification_deadline_line_format~
writes each of its lines, ~%c: %D %a~ unless set, where ~%a~ is the
headline as the agenda shows it and ~%w~ the deadline's time, if it has
one:

#+begin_src toml
notification_deadline_line_format = "%D %h (%c)"
#+end_src

#+begin_example
Deadlines
2 d. ago: Taxes (work)
In 3 d.: Report (work)
#+end_example

* Trying it
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: trying-it
:END:

#+begin_src sh
org-lsp notify --dry-run --now "2026-10-22 09:48"
org-lsp notify --dry-run --now "2026-10-22 08:30" --options '{ "notification_deadline_summary_time": "08:30" }'
org-lsp notify --help
#+end_src

~--dry-run~ prints each notification, its title and then its text, and
sends none; ~--now~ pretends a date and time. As ~org-lsp agenda~, it
takes ~--options~ over the configuration file's, ~--no-config~ for none,
and files to look at in place of the agenda files.

* What differs from Emacs
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: what-differs-from-emacs
:END:

- ~appt.el~ keeps its appointments and counts its reminders from the first
  one it showed; ~org-lsp notify~ keeps nothing, and reminds of each
  appointment on its own at the minutes ~appt_message_warning_time~ and
  ~appt_display_interval~ give. Two appointments due a minute apart are
  reminded of at the minutes of each, where Emacs reminds of both at the
  minutes of the first.
- Emacs makes the appointments once, when ~org-agenda-to-appt~ runs; a
  change to the files shows the next minute here.
- The list of minutes of ~appt_message_warning_time~, the deadlines of
  the day and the formats of titles, texts and lines are org-lsp's own.
- Whole hours are said as hours: ~Appointment in 1 hour~,
  ~Appointments in 1,2 hours~, ~1h: 11:00 Doctor~, where ~appt.el~ says
  ~in 60 minutes~. Other times are minutes, as in Emacs.
- ~notification_use_timestamp_warning~ is org-lsp's own: ~appt.el~
  reads no warning cookie, and Org counts them in days, so that ~-1h~ is
  no warning at all (~org-get-wdays~ takes an hour for 0.041667 days and
  rounds down).
- ~appt-display-format~, ~appt-display-duration~, ~appt-audible~ and the
  mode line are the desktop's business: how long a notification stays and
  whether it makes a sound.
- Appointments from the diary file (~appt-check~ reads it too) are not
  read.
- An ~APPT_WARNTIME~ that is not an integer, as ~30.5~, makes ~appt-add~
  fail with ~Argument WARNTIME must be an integer~, and
  ~org-agenda-to-appt~ with it, for every appointment. Here its leading
  integer is taken (30), as ~string-to-number~ reads it, and a number too
  large for one is 0.
