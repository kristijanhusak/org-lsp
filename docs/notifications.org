#+title: Notifications
#+options: ^:{} toc:1

Reminders of the day's appointments, with no editor open, as Emacs's
~appt.el~ gives them once ~org-agenda-to-appt~ has made the appointments
of the agenda files. ~org-lsp notify~ looks at the agenda files and sends
the reminders due that minute; the system runs it each minute (cron, a
systemd timer, launchd, the Task Scheduler). It keeps nothing between runs.

#+begin_example
Appointment in 12 minutes
10:00 TODO Standup                            :meeting:
#+end_example

* Setting it up
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: setting-it-up
:END:

#+begin_src sh
scripts/install.sh notifications
# or, without a checkout:
curl -fsSL https://github.com/kristijanhusak/org-lsp/releases/download/nightly/install.sh | bash -s -- notifications
# and to take it away again:
scripts/install.sh --remove notifications
#+end_src

The server goes in =~/.local/bin= first if it is not there
(~ORG_LSP_BIN_DIR~ for another directory). The agenda files are those of
the shared configuration file, =~/.config/org-lsp/config.toml= (~org-lsp
config~ shows which and what it says):

#+begin_src toml
org_agenda_files = ["~/org"]
#+end_src

What the script makes, which can be made by hand as well:

- Linux, with systemd :: a user timer,
  =~/.config/systemd/user/org-lsp-notify.timer=, that runs
  =org-lsp-notify.service= at the start of each minute:

  #+begin_src conf
  [Service]
  Type=oneshot
  ExecStart="/home/me/.local/bin/org-lsp" notify

  [Timer]
  OnCalendar=*-*-* *:*:00
  AccuracySec=1s
  #+end_src

  ~systemctl --user list-timers~ shows it, ~journalctl --user -u
  org-lsp-notify~ what it said. ~notify-send~ needs the session's bus: if
  no notification comes, ~systemctl --user import-environment DISPLAY
  WAYLAND_DISPLAY DBUS_SESSION_BUS_ADDRESS~ in the session's startup gives
  the timer what it needs.
- Linux, without systemd :: a line of the crontab (~crontab -l~), added
  once:

  #+begin_src sh
  ,* * * * * DBUS_SESSION_BUS_ADDRESS=unix:path=/run/user/1000/bus '/home/me/.local/bin/org-lsp' notify
  #+end_src

  Cron gives its commands little of the session: the address of the
  session's bus is there for ~notify-send~. ~1000~ is your user's number
  (~id -u~).
- macOS :: a LaunchAgent, =~/Library/LaunchAgents/org.org-lsp.notify.plist=,
  that runs ~org-lsp notify~ at the start of each minute (a
  ~StartCalendarInterval~ with no fields), loaded with ~launchctl
  bootstrap~. The notifications are those of ~osascript~ (Script Editor's
  in System Settings > Notifications).
- Windows :: a scheduled task, ~org-lsp-notify~, each minute
  (~schtasks /SC MINUTE~). It runs
  =%LOCALAPPDATA%\org-lsp\notify.vbs= with ~wscript~, which starts
  ~org-lsp.exe notify~ without a window. The notifications are toasts of
  [[https://github.com/Windos/BurntToast][BurntToast]] when that PowerShell module is installed, else of
  Windows's own, in the name of PowerShell.

Elsewhere, run ~org-lsp notify~ each minute in any way the system has.

* What it reminds of
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: what-it-reminds-of
:END:

The appointments are what ~org-agenda-to-appt~ takes with its defaults:
the entries of today's agenda that have a time of day, from a scheduled
date, a deadline or a timestamp, with repeaters and diary sexps in
timestamps on the days they fall; a time range at its start. Entries in a
done state are left out, as Org leaves out any whose text has a done
keyword and no other. ~scripts/appt_reference.py~ records the appointments
Emacs makes of the files of =tests/appt=, and the tests compare.

Each is reminded of ~appt_message_warning_time~ minutes before it (12),
then each ~appt_display_interval~ minutes (3) until it is due: at 9:48,
9:51, 9:54, 9:57 and 10:00 for a meeting at 10:00. An entry's
~APPT_WARNTIME~ property gives its own minutes. A list gives the minutes
of each reminder instead:

#+begin_src toml
appt_message_warning_time = [30, 5, 0]
#+end_src

Appointments due together come in one notification, as ~appt.el~ shows
them in one window: its mode line (~Appointment in 12 minutes~,
~Appointments in 5,10 minutes~, ~Appointment now~) is the title, and each
appointment a line of the text, its time and its headline, after the
minutes when they differ (~5m: 10:00 Standup~).

~notification_match~ keeps the entries a match string of the tags view
matches, as ~org-lsp agenda --view tags --match~ takes it:

#+begin_src toml
notification_match = "+meeting|+call"
#+end_src

** The deadlines of the day
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: the-deadlines-of-the-day
:END:

With ~notification_deadline_summary_time~, once a day, at that time, one
notification lists the deadlines today's agenda shows that are not done:
due today, coming within their warning period (~org_deadline_warning_days~,
or the timestamp's own ~-3d~), and past, each with its category and the
agenda's words for it:

#+begin_src toml
notification_deadline_summary_time = "08:30"
#+end_src

#+begin_example
Deadlines
work: 2 d. ago: TODO Taxes
work: In 3 d.: TODO Report
#+end_example

No deadlines, no notification.

* Sending them elsewhere
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: sending-them-elsewhere
:END:

~notification_command~ is a program and its arguments, run for each
notification in place of the desktop's, with ~%t~ for the title and ~%b~
for the text. No shell reads it. To a phone with [[https://ntfy.sh][ntfy]]:

#+begin_src toml
notification_command = ["ntfy", "publish", "--title", "%t", "my-org-reminders", "%b"]
#+end_src

or with ~curl~:

#+begin_src toml
notification_command = ["curl", "-fsS", "-H", "Title: %t", "-d", "%b", "https://ntfy.sh/my-org-reminders"]
#+end_src

With ~notification_desktop = true~ the desktop's comes too. A program that
fails, or takes longer than 30 seconds, is said on standard error, and the
command ends with status 1.

* Trying it
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: trying-it
:END:

#+begin_src sh
org-lsp notify --dry-run --now "2026-10-22 09:48"
org-lsp notify --dry-run --now "2026-10-22 08:30" --options '{ "notification_deadline_summary_time": "08:30" }'
org-lsp notify --help
#+end_src

~--dry-run~ prints each notification, its title and then its text, and
sends none; ~--now~ pretends a date and time. As ~org-lsp agenda~, it
takes ~--options~ over the configuration file's, ~--no-config~ for none,
and files to look at in place of the agenda files.

* What differs from Emacs
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: what-differs-from-emacs
:END:

- ~appt.el~ keeps its appointments and counts its reminders from the first
  one it showed; ~org-lsp notify~ keeps nothing, and reminds of each
  appointment on its own at the minutes ~appt_message_warning_time~ and
  ~appt_display_interval~ give. Two appointments due a minute apart are
  reminded of at the minutes of each, where Emacs reminds of both at the
  minutes of the first.
- Emacs makes the appointments once, when ~org-agenda-to-appt~ runs; a
  change to the files shows the next minute here.
- The list of minutes of ~appt_message_warning_time~ and the deadlines of
  the day are org-lsp's own.
- ~appt-display-format~, ~appt-display-duration~, ~appt-audible~ and the
  mode line are the desktop's business: how long a notification stays and
  whether it makes a sound.
- Appointments from the diary file (~appt-check~ reads it too) are not
  read.
